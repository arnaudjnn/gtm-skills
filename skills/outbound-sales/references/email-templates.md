# Cold email templates

Five templates, each keyed to a signal type. Pick by signal, fill the brackets, scrub against the anti-AI rules below, ship.

## Universal rules

- **Subject ≤ 8 words.** No "Quick question", "Following up", "Touching base", "Re:".
- **Body ≤ 90 words.** Anything longer reads as marketing.
- **First sentence references the signal**, not the company generally.
- **One ask, one CTA.** No "happy to send a deck OR book a call OR jump on Zoom".
- **No em dashes.** No "Hope this finds you well." No "I wanted to reach out because…".
- **Sign off plain.** "— <name>" or first name only. No quotes-under-name, no titles in sig.

## Template 1 — Hiring-Sales-Rep-Repost

**Subject:** "SDR re-listing — Gymshark"

```
{First name},

Saw the SDR role got re-listed at {Company} — the original posting was
{N} weeks ago. Usually means the last hire didn't make it past ramp.

We work with {2-3 named peer companies}; the common pattern was the
playbook the SDRs were given was the same one that worked at half the
size. {One sentence on what changed for them, with a number}.

Worth a 15-min compare on what they fixed?

— {Your first name}
```

Anti-pattern: don't say "I noticed your SDR role". The signal is the repost specifically, not the hiring.

## Template 2 — Trustpilot Negative Support Reviews

**Subject:** "{N} Trustpilot reviews about support"

```
{First name},

Saw {N} negative Trustpilot reviews about {Company}'s support response
time in the last 30 days. The common thread was {specific theme from
the reviews — e.g. "48+ hour first-reply" or "duplicate ticket creation"}.

Usually means ticket volume jumped before headcount did. The {function}
teams we work with at your scale cut first-reply to under 6h without
doubling the CX headcount. {Named peer if you have one}.

Worth a 15-min compare on what changed for them?

— {Your first name}
```

Anti-pattern: don't quote the reviews verbatim — paraphrase the theme. Verbatim quoting reads as scraping.

## Template 3 — Hiring Support / CX

**Subject:** "{N} CX roles, ramp question"

```
{First name},

Saw the {N} CX roles {Company} posted in the last 14 days. Congrats —
that's a real budget unlock.

The thing most teams underweight at this hiring pace is ramp tooling.
{Named peer} brought 8 new CX hires to first-solo-resolution in {2.5
weeks} instead of {6} — same hiring profile, different onboarding stack.

Worth me sending the {asset/playbook} they used?

— {Your first name}
```

Anti-pattern: don't pitch "we'll replace your CX team". The recipient is excited to be growing the team — pitch tooling that compounds the new hires, not removes them.

## Template 4 — Socials Spike

**Subject:** "+{X}% on {platform} — operational side"

```
{First name},

Saw {Company}'s {Instagram/TikTok} jumped {X}% in the last 30 days.
That's the marketing side firing.

What usually breaks next is the operational backbone — fulfillment or
CX, depending on the brand. {Named peer that hit a similar spike}
prepped for it by {specific thing}.

Worth a 15-min trade-notes on what they wished they'd done sooner?

— {Your first name}
```

Anti-pattern: don't congratulate without a useful angle. "Great growth!" is a delete. The pitch is "the next mountain", not "well done".

## Template 5 — Technology Stack (qualification + timing)

**Subject:** "{Tech X} stack — {role}"

```
{First name},

Saw {Company} runs {Tech X}. Most of the {function} teams I work with
came from {Tech X} after they hit {specific friction — usually a scale
threshold, not a feature gap}.

{Named peer} switched at {scale point} and saw {specific outcome with
a number}.

Worth a 15-min on what their evaluation criteria were?

— {Your first name}
```

Anti-pattern: don't lead with the tool ("we're better than Tech X"). Lead with the friction the recipient is likely already feeling, then mention the migration.

## What changes by industry / persona

The templates above are scaffolds. Two things vary by recipient:

1. **The "{Named peer}" placeholder.** Use a real customer in their tier (similar revenue range or similar company stage). Generic ("our customers") is a delete.
2. **The CTA verb.** "Worth a quick compare" beats "Would love to chat" for sales-side personas. "Want me to send X" beats "Worth a call" for operational personas (CX, RevOps).

## Sign-off conventions

Plain text:

```
— First
```

OR

```
First
```

Not:

```
First Lastname
Title
Company  | Website  | Phone
```

Long signatures are a delete-tell. Recipients judge salesy/spammy on the signature before reading the body.

## Follow-up cadence (if user asks)

This skill drafts the FIRST email. Follow-ups (2nd, 3rd, breakup) are typically generated by the user's outreach platform via sequence templates. If the user wants a 3-touch sequence drafted here, ask explicitly and produce:

- **Touch 1** — signal-keyed (the templates above)
- **Touch 2** (D+3): one-line reference back to T1 + a different angle (use the second-priority signal that fired, or a piece of social proof not yet used). ≤ 50 words.
- **Touch 3** (D+7, "breakup"): permission-to-close. "Tried twice, no movement on your end — fine to close the loop here? Happy to drop a note when X changes." ≤ 40 words.

Don't over-write follow-ups. Reps hate getting templates that feel longer with each touch.
