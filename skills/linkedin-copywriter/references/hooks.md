# LinkedIn hook templates

The hook is the first 210 characters — everything above LinkedIn's "…see more" fold. If the hook doesn't earn a click-to-expand, the post is dead regardless of body quality. Use the 3-step formula or one of the 10 templates.

## The 3-step formula

Every strong LinkedIn hook follows this structure:

1. **Context Lean-In** — one short, declarative line that gives just enough context to anchor the reader. Often a number, a date, or a named thing.
2. **Scroll Stop Interjection** — a one-line pivot that breaks the expected continuation. A question, a contradiction, a confession.
3. **Contrarian Snapback** — the line that earns the click. The point you're about to defend in the body.

Example:
```
Just hired our 30th engineer at $5M ARR.
Half the playbooks said wait until $10M.
Both playbooks were wrong.
```

## 10 hook templates (with engagement signal)

### F1 — Anaphora ("Same line, three times")

Same opening words, three different endings. Repetition cadence stops the scroll.

```
We tried writing posts ourselves. We tried ghostwriters. We tried automation.
None of them moved the needle until we changed one thing.
```

### F2 — R.I.P. (Obituary)

Declare a category dead. Maximally contrarian.

```
R.I.P. cold email open rates above 30%.
2026 was the year inbox AI quietly killed the metric we built our funnels on.
```

### F3 — Year-pivot

Anchor on a year + a transformation.

```
2024: I sent 200 cold emails for one meeting.
2026: I send 8 LinkedIn comments for the same meeting.
The math is brutal.
```

### F4 — Time-anchor

Specific time-window + specific outcome.

```
Three hours after pushing the new pricing page, we lost two trial signups
and gained one $48k deal.
```

### F5 — Self-proving

The post itself is evidence of the claim.

```
This is the third post I've written in 11 minutes.
None of them used the word "leverage" or the em dash.
None of them passed an AI-detector check until I broke them on purpose.
```

### F6 — Paid-vs-Free

Two paths, one obviously worse, the contrarian take that the worse one wins.

```
Free: spend 4 hours/day commenting on LinkedIn.
Paid: spend $5k/month on a ghostwriter.
The free one outperformed the paid one 7-to-1.
```

### F7 — Curiosity-gap

Open a loop the body has to close. Don't tease vaguely — tease concretely.

```
Our top-performing post last quarter had 142 comments.
Zero of them came from people who'd ever heard of our product.
Here's why that worked.
```

### F8 — Odd-money

Specific odd dollar number. Round numbers feel made up; odd numbers feel real.

```
We just refunded $11,847 to one customer.
Best money our CS team spent this year.
```

### F9 — Contrarian (general)

Pure inversion of a widely held belief.

```
Hiring for "culture fit" is the single fastest way to kill a startup's
distribution. Hire for thesis fit instead.
```

### F10 — Comment-gate

Frame the body around a question that forces a comment to fully resolve.

```
The thing nobody tells you about scaling a CX team past 20 people:
the playbook that got you here is the exact thing that breaks at 30.
What broke first for you?
```

## Common hook anti-patterns (don't use)

- "Here's the thing about [X]:" — banned (see anti-ai.md)
- "Let me tell you a story" — too long a wind-up
- "Picture this:" — overused
- "I used to think X. Now I think Y." — overused; use F1 anaphora instead
- "Here are 5 lessons I learned…" — listicle-without-substance signal
- Question-only openers ("Why do most startups fail?") — algorithm down-ranks pure-question posts
- Emoji openers — read as engagement-bait
